What am I looking at?

Columns and rows

Each word you or Settle write becomes a column of cells. Each row reaches a different distance back: the bottom row looks one or two words back, every row above twice as far. The top rows hold the big picture.

Settling is thinking

A new column updates again and again, listening to its neighbours, until it stops changing. A bright outline means it is still moving. Hard words take more steps; the bars underneath count them.

The lens (pencil, not ink)

Once a column settles it is fixed, like ink. Then Settle re-reads the last few words both ways in a scratch copy, so the newest word can reshape how the previous ones are read. Then the pencil is erased.

Links and gist

Arcs show which earlier words the column reached back to, for example a character's name. The thin strip is the gist: a slow-moving summary of the mood of the whole text.

The story

Your words are white, Settle's are warm. A brighter glow means Settle thought longer about that word than about the others.
